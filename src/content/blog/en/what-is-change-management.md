Change management is the structured way of guiding people through a change, so that a new way of working takes hold in daily work and lasts. Prosci, whose method I rely on, defines it as [the application of a structured process and set of tools for leading the people side of change](https://prosci.com/blog/what-is-change-management-and-how-does-it-work) to achieve a desired outcome. Change management is therefore measured by whether the people affected actually work differently afterwards. New software, a new process or a new organizational structure has only been introduced once that is the case.

## What exactly is change management?

Every change in a company has two sides. The technical side is the solution itself: the new system, the new process, the new structure. The people side is whether the people expected to use the solution accept it. Change management addresses the second side.

There are two perspectives that belong together:

- **The individual.** Every person experiences change individually: they need to understand why something is changing, want to take part, know how it works, be able to apply it in daily work and stick with it. Prosci's [ADKAR model](https://www.prosci.com/blog/adkar-model) describes exactly these five stages.
- **The organization.** A company needs a plan that covers every group affected: who hears what and when, who sponsors the change, how training and communication fit together and how success is measured.

Typical triggers are new software such as a CRM or ERP system, new processes in sales or service, a reorganization, a move to a new site or a merger.

## How does change management differ from project management?

The three terms are often confused, especially in IT projects. They answer different questions:

| Discipline | Key question | Result when it works |
|---|---|---|
| Project management | Is the solution delivered on time, on budget and in the agreed quality? | The solution is delivered |
| Change management | Do people accept the solution and work with it? | The solution is used |
| Change Enablement (ITIL 4, formerly IT change management) | Is a change to a system assessed, approved and scheduled in a controlled way? | The systems stay stable |

Project management and change management run in parallel and need each other. A project can finish on time and on budget and still fail, because after go-live everyone keeps working with their old lists. IT change management, which ITIL 4 now calls Change Enablement, is a separate topic: it assesses risks, approves changes and schedules them for IT products and services. Anyone searching for "IT change management" usually finds this topic.

Prosci's research shows how much organizational change management matters. Since 1998, the institute has surveyed participants about their experience with change in projects. In the executive summary of the 12th edition of its study [Best Practices in Change Management](https://www.prosci.com/hubfs/Website_English/2.downloads/research-executive-summaries/Best-Practices-in-Change-Management-12th-Edition-Executive-Summary.pdf), projects with excellent change management met their objectives about seven times as often as projects with poor change management. The underlying chart is based on Prosci's cumulative benchmarking data from more than 10,800 respondents; the 12th edition itself had 2,668 participants.

```kennzahlen
88 % | Projects with excellent change management that meet or exceed their objectives
13 % | Projects with poor change management that meet or exceed their objectives
10,800+ | Respondents in Prosci's cumulative benchmarking data
source: Prosci, Best Practices in Change Management, 12th edition executive summary (2023)
```

## Prosci divides change management into three phases

At the level of the organization, Prosci divides the work into three phases in the [Prosci 3-Phase Process](https://www.prosci.com/methodology/3-phase-process). They run alongside the project plan, not after it:

```schritte
Prepare approach | before the start · sponsor and change manager
  Define what changes for which group, who in management sponsors the change, which risks exist and how success is measured.
Manage change | during delivery · managers and change manager
  Carry out a plan per group: communication, sponsor appearances, training, coaching. Check regularly where it is stuck and adjust.
Sustain outcomes | after the start · managers and internal owners
  Measure whether the new way of working is used, close gaps, make successes visible and anchor ownership inside the company for good.
```

A common and costly mistake occurs in the first phase: change management starts too late. When communication begins two weeks before go-live, it can only inform. Identifying who is affected and what changes for each group belongs at the beginning, because it also shapes the solution itself. The tool for this is the [stakeholder analysis](../stakeholder-analysis/), from which the [communication plan](../change-management-communication-plan/) follows.

At the level of the individual, the ADKAR model helps diagnose the issue. When a rollout stalls, one of the five stages is usually missing, and each needs a different measure. Another training session only helps when knowledge or ability is missing. When nobody has understood why the change is necessary, the manager has to explain it. The article [change management models compared](../change-management-models/) explains how ADKAR differs from other well-known models.

## Sponsor, managers, change team and key users share the work

Change management is teamwork with clearly assigned roles:

- **The sponsor.** A person from management who visibly backs the change, makes decisions and allocates resources. In the Prosci study, participants name active and visible sponsorship as the biggest contributor to success.
- **Direct managers.** They translate the change into the daily work of their team, answer the question "what does this mean for me?" and are the first to notice resistance.
- **The change manager.** Plans the approach, advises sponsor and managers, prepares communication and training and measures progress. They do not bear sole responsibility for the outcome; that responsibility remains with the company.
- **Multipliers.** In software rollouts, these are usually [key users](../salesforce-key-user-network/): colleagues from the business who are involved early and answer the first questions in day-to-day work.

In Germany, there is one more role that international guides often omit: the works council. Under [section 87 (1) no. 6 of the Works Constitution Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html) (German text) it has a right of co-determination when technical equipment is introduced that is designed to monitor the behavior or performance of employees. According to the Federal Labor Court, a system need only be objectively capable of recording individual behavior or performance, and many business systems record which user performed which action and when. Therefore, check this early for every new system. Involving the works council early does not guarantee agreement, but it allows rules on data and monitoring to be designed before go-live; addressing it late can delay the project by months.

## What does change management look like when software is introduced?

Take the introduction of a CRM system such as Salesforce in a company with field sales, inside sales and service. From a technical perspective, the system is ready after a few months. Whether people use it depends on other factors:

1. **Write down groups and changes.** Document for each group what changes in daily work. Field sales record visit reports in the app instead of a spreadsheet, inside sales create quotes in the system instead of in Word.
2. **Make the sponsor visible.** The head of sales explains in a team meeting why forecasts will be generated in the system and follows up on this in the monthly report.
3. **Check the ADKAR stage per group.** Inside sales are willing to take part but do not know the new steps yet: training on real cases helps. Field sales see no benefit: a conversation with their manager helps, another training session will not change anything.
4. **Measure usage.** What counts is whether records are created and maintained; the number of training sessions says little about that. Reports in the system show it more reliably than a survey.

Two projects from my work show the range: a global Salesforce Sales Cloud rollout with more than 500 users in the US, Asia and Europe, and an IT transformation at an insurer with more than 20,000 people affected in 19 countries. The phases are the same; the plan for each group and country becomes more demanding as the size increases. How I set this up with Fabian Kramer for Salesforce programs is described in [Salesforce change management: why rollouts fail](../change-management-salesforce-rollout/).

## Outside help pays off once several groups or countries are affected

Not every change needs an external change manager. If only one process changes for a team of ten, a manager who uses ADKAR as a checklist and answers the five questions honestly is enough.

Outside support is worthwhile when several groups or countries are affected, when no one internally has the time and experience to plan it, or when the sponsor is still unclear. Then an outside view helps clarify roles, coach the managers and hand the work over to internal owners at the end.
