Salesforce CPQ is end of sale, not end of life. New customers can no longer buy it. Existing customers can keep using CPQ under their current contracts, buy additional users, renew and receive support and critical fixes. New features will not come, and Salesforce has announced no end-of-life date and says there is no forced migration. For the Salesforce Billing package there is no comparable statement on a Salesforce page, only a spokesperson's assurance of full access. So Salesforce sets no migration deadline, but the time before the next CPQ renewal is the best window to secure the conditions for a later move to Revenue Cloud.

## What exactly has Salesforce announced for CPQ?

The primary public source is the Salesforce page [Navigating the Future of Salesforce CPQ](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/cpq/end-of-life/). It describes Salesforce's product position; your order form and contract still decide what you may buy and at what price. As of November 2026 it states:

- "Salesforce CPQ is end of sale, not end of life."
- Existing customers "can continue using Salesforce CPQ, buy additional users, renew licenses, and receive support", elsewhere on the page with the addition "under their current contracts".
- CPQ is in a maintenance phase: "existing customers continue to receive support and critical fixes", but CPQ "will not receive new features or innovations".
- "Salesforce has not announced an end-of-life date for CPQ."
- "There is no forced migration" to the new product.
- About 15 percent of Revenue Cloud Advanced customers migrated from CPQ, and a typical migration runs three to six months from discovery to go-live. The page adds that complex implementations can take longer, which is the normal case for a manufacturer with a configurator, custom pricing and ERP integration.

The press release on [Agentforce for Revenue](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentforce-for-revenue-announcement/) from 16 July 2025 adds that Salesforce "remains committed to supporting current Salesforce CPQ customers" and that it "rebuilt its CPQ solution as the all-new Revenue Cloud". That last sentence explains the situation: Revenue Cloud is the successor, and Salesforce built it as a new product rather than as a new CPQ version.

## Existing customers renew, add users and get support

In practice the status means:

| Question | Answer for existing CPQ customers |
|---|---|
| Keep using CPQ in production | Yes |
| Buy additional CPQ users | Yes |
| Renew the CPQ contract | Yes, conditions are negotiated at renewal |
| Support and critical fixes | Yes |
| New features | No |
| End-of-life date | None announced |
| Forced migration | None |
| New CPQ for a new company or subsidiary org | Not answered by the public statement; get it confirmed in the order form |

```hinweis offen
The last row is my own reading. Whether a new legal entity or a new org counts as an existing customer depends on the contracting entity and the order form. A group that wants to open a new org for a subsidiary should get that confirmed in writing before it signs. I would not assume it.
```

What "no new features" means over time is easy to underestimate. Salesforce's new development in quote to cash goes into Revenue Cloud, release by release, and CPQ stays where it is. The Agentforce quoting functions that Salesforce announced in July 2025, for example, are part of Revenue Cloud. A CPQ org does not stop working, it just falls further behind the product Salesforce is actively building.

## What applies to Salesforce Billing?

Here the statements are thinner, and I would keep to exactly what is published. The CPQ page does not mention the Salesforce Billing package at all. It names Revenue Cloud Billing as the billing product of the new suite.

The only statement on Billing comes from a Salesforce spokesperson quoted by [SalesforceBen](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-confirms-the-future-of-cpq/) in March 2025: "Current Salesforce CPQ & Salesforce Billing customers will continue to receive full access", and new customers are directed to Revenue Cloud Advanced and Revenue Cloud Billing.

Whether the Billing package is still sold to new customers is not stated on any Salesforce page I found. So I would not assume that every CPQ assurance, such as buying more users or renewing, applies to Billing in the same way; get it confirmed in writing at the renewal. What is clear: existing customers keep running it, and Salesforce develops billing in Revenue Cloud Billing. If invoices come from the Billing package today, the move is a finance project as much as a CRM project. The details are in the article on [moving from Salesforce Billing to Revenue Cloud Billing](../from-salesforce-billing-to-revenue-cloud-billing/).

## Where does the date March 2025 come from?

Many articles say Salesforce stopped selling CPQ in March 2025. That date is reported by third parties such as the SalesforceBen article quoted above, updated on 27 March 2025. The Salesforce page itself names neither an end-of-sale date nor an end-of-life date. If you cite a date in a board paper or a tender, mark it as reported.

The background helps when partners use different names. Salesforce CPQ and Salesforce Billing are managed packages with the namespaces `SBQQ` and `blng`. For the successor, Salesforce Help now says Revenue Management (formerly Revenue Cloud) or Agentforce Revenue Management, while the licenses are still called Revenue Cloud Growth, Revenue Cloud Advanced and Revenue Cloud Billing. I use Revenue Cloud here because that is the name on the order form.

## Secure term, price cap and parallel running at the next renewal

For me the time before the renewal is the most important decision window, because that is when you still have something to negotiate with. Before you sign, clarify these points in writing:

1. **Term.** A longer term gives planning certainty if the migration is two or three years away. A shorter one keeps flexibility if Revenue Cloud is already on the roadmap. Decide which you need before the account team proposes one.
2. **Price increase cap.** For a product without new features, I would not accept open-ended increases at the next renewals.
3. **Parallel operation.** Salesforce's [migration guide](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?language=en_US&id=ind.rev_data_migration.htm&type=5) lists "Retain CPQ License" and "Coexistence During Transition" as planning topics. A migration runs CPQ and Revenue Cloud side by side for a while. The guide describes this operationally; it does not define the commercial terms for licensing both products. Those you negotiate, so the contract should allow both without paying twice for every user for longer than necessary.
4. **User growth.** If you expect more quoting users, agree the price for additional CPQ users now.
5. **Billing.** If you also run the Billing package, renew it together with CPQ and on the same terms, so the two products do not end up with different notice periods.

A worked example: a machine builder with 60 CPQ users renews in spring 2027 and expects to move to Revenue Cloud in about two years. A two-year term with a cap on increases and a written option to run Revenue Cloud next to CPQ for six months fits this plan. A three-year term without that option would force a decision at the wrong time. How I prepare such negotiations is described under [make-or-buy and license forecasts](../../services/make-or-buy-licences/), and which users need which license at all is covered in [Salesforce licenses: does everyone need a full one?](../salesforce-licences-full-licence-needed/).

## Does end of sale mean you should move now?

No. The status removes the assumption of an imminent shutdown by Salesforce, and I would use that time. Your own deadlines, such as the renewal date or an ERP program, still apply. Whether staying, moving or a third route fits depends on your processes, your pricing master and your budget, and I go through the criteria in [keep Salesforce CPQ or move to Revenue Cloud?](../keep-salesforce-cpq-or-revenue-cloud/). For manufacturers who are only now choosing a quoting system, CPQ is no longer an option at all. They compare Revenue Cloud, partner products and a custom approach, as described in [from quote to sales agreement](../quote-to-sales-agreement-product-configurator/).
